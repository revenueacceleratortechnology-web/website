How RA Tech Builds an Amazon Design Project
A clear production path keeps design decisions in the right order. The Amazon design services workflow moves through planning, direction, production, and approval.
Project Discovery
The project starts with the problem the creative needs to solve. We clarify the intended customer, marketplace context, decision-makers, and approval path. The discussion defines the questions design must answer. It does not set technical specifications or final production details.
Information Mapping
We rank the information by importance before visual planning. Product facts, brand messages, proof points, and supporting details receive a clear order. Competing messages should not crowd the same visual space. Only approved information moves into the creative plan.
Creative Direction
The creative direction sets the visual language for the project. Reference styles, typography, imagery, graphic treatment, and layout behavior are reviewed together. Existing brand rules remain intact where suitable. Any proposed departure needs a clear design reason and client approval.
Structure and Wireframes
Wireframes establish placement before finished artwork begins. They show the weight and order of key content without polished styling. Both teams review hierarchy, balance, and space before production. Production starts only after the structure is approved.
Design Production
Approved structure moves into finished creative. Designers apply final imagery, copy, graphics, and approved brand elements to the selected layouts. Production follows the agreed direction rather than reopening earlier decisions. New issues are flagged when they affect accuracy or execution.
Client Review and Finalization
Review focuses on factual accuracy, visual hierarchy, brand fit, and approved project intent. Feedback is consolidated before changes are applied. Accepted revisions move into the final creative version. Client approval then closes the design-production stage.
Built for Amazon Requirements, Mobile, and Submission
Creative approval does not make an asset ready for publication. Amazon applies format, content, and placement rules across different creative surfaces. A separate technical pass covers the work before handoff or implementation.
Amazon Specifications
Product imagery follows current file and quality requirements before upload. Supported formats include JPEG, TIFF, PNG, and non-animated GIF files. The longest side can range from 500 to 10,000 pixels. At least one image is required, and six or more are recommended. Main-image checks also cover pure-white backgrounds and product fill where required.
Mobile Presentation
Brand stores need separate mobile review because desktop layouts can crop or reorder. Custom mobile images are available for image tiles and header images. They are not available for every store widget. We check text legibility, safe areas, crop behavior, content order, and navigation. Final previews are reviewed on smaller screens.
Content Compliance
Store creative must meet Amazon content acceptance policies before publication. Claims need support where the applicable policy requires it. Images, text, links, and video are checked against placement rules. Store videos cannot exceed five minutes. Locale language requirements also apply during moderation. Unsupported promotional language is removed before submission.
Final QA and Submission Readiness
Final checks cover rendering, links, page relationships, file exports, and missing assets. Store navigation is tested before submission. Desktop and mobile previews are reviewed separately. Moderation generally finishes within 24 hours, but review can take three business days. Final handoff follows the agreed implementation scope. Rejected items return for correction.
Test and Review Amazon Creative With Performance Data
Eligible creative decisions are testable through Amazon's available experimentation tools. Store performance remains visible after publication through store insights. These signals give an Amazon creative agency evidence for selected design decisions. Wider commercial factors still need separate consideration.
Creative Experiments
Manage your experiments supports eligible image, A+ content, and brand story tests. Shoppers are split between content versions during the experiment. Reported metrics include sales, units sold, conversion rate, units per unique visitor, and sample size. Eligible products also need enough recent traffic.
Store Insights
Brand store insights reports performance by page and traffic source. Available measures include daily visitors, page views, visits, attributed sales, and related engagement data. The dashboard reveals traffic entry points and page-level attention. Source tags separate traffic from selected external marketing activity.
Decisions After Launch
Post-launch data gives context for later creative decisions. Weak performance does not automatically point to design. Traffic quality, price, reviews, demand, inventory, and the offer also influence results. Available evidence narrows the next creative question. Approved assets change only when the test has a clear purpose.
